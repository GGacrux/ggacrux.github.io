# Publishing on GGacrux Research

Create a Markdown file in `_posts/` using the filename format `YYYY-MM-DD-short-title.md`. Add the front matter below, write your article, and commit it to the repository's main branch. GitHub Pages builds the article page and adds it to the journal automatically.

```yaml
---
title: "Your article title"
subtitle: "Optional one-line framing"
date: YYYY-MM-DD
category: Research
tags:
  - Artificial Intelligence
  - Research
summary: "A concise summary for the publication index."
source: "Optional organization, paper, or project"
author: "GGacrux"
# Optional:
# hero_image: /assets/images/your-image.jpg
# hero_image_alt: "Accessible image description"
# image_caption: "Image credit or context"
# sources:
#   - title: "Original paper or source"
#     publisher: "Publisher"
#     url: "https://example.com"
---
```

## A flexible article outline

Use the sections that suit the piece; this is a guide, not a rigid template.

1. **The question** — what caught your attention and what are you trying to understand?
2. **Context** — what happened, what did you read, and why does it matter?
3. **What the sources say** — important claims, evidence, and original links.
4. **My investigation** — tests, comparisons, observations, or research process.
5. **Findings** — what you learned; separate evidence from interpretation.
6. **My perspective** — what you agree with, question, or think is missing.
7. **Open questions** — what remains uncertain or deserves more work.
8. **References** — link original papers, reports, documentation, and announcements.

## Categories

- `Research`: independent investigation, analysis, or synthesis.
- `Briefing`: a considered briefing on a paper, release, or development.
- `Field Notes`: observations, experiments, and work in progress.
- `Experience`: practical lessons and personal technical experience.

Be clear about what is sourced, what you personally observed, and what is your interpretation. Link primary sources wherever possible.
