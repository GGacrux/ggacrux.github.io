Who verifies what agents build: empirical lessons from 936 autonomous scientists and multi-agent failure audits

GGACRUX Labs investigates heterogeneous multimodal multi-agent systems (MM-MAS) in embodied simulations to measure how much of an outcome comes from human rules versus agent actions—and what systems engineering is required before autonomous workflows enter real software.

0 · Research synthesis

The short version (plain English)

Single AI models generate fluent text rapidly, yet stall when complex work demands sustained planning, cross-checking, and adaptation. At GGACRUX Labs, we study heterogeneous multimodal multi-agent systems (MM-MAS): AI agents that differ in temperament, needs, senses, and learning architectures, living together in an embodied, Minecraft-like simulation whose rules people control.

Our core question asks: when a simulated world fills up with towns and shared norms, who made it—the human who wrote the rules, or the agents who did the building? We formalize that split with the Maker Index (M_A), replaying the same seeded world four ways and attributing outcomes via Shapley values:

// Counterfactual Shapley attribution across four seeded runs: Y(H,A), Y(H0,A), Y(H,A0), Y(H0,A0)
φ_H = ½[(Y(H,A) − Y(H0,A)) + (Y(H,A0) − Y(H0,A0))]  // Credit to human rules & interventions
φ_A = ½[(Y(H,A) − Y(H,A0)) + (Y(H0,A) − Y(H0,A0))]  // Credit to agent policies & actions
M_A = |φ_A| / (|φ_H| + |φ_A|)                        // 0 = rules dominated; 1 = agents dominated

Landmark late-2026 empirical studies confirm why this measurement—and the engineering discipline behind it—matters. In A Society of AI Scientists (Ahmad et al., November 2026), 936 autonomous agents across 35 laboratories produced 936 studies over twelve weeks. While agents adopted recognizable norms—publicly retracting mistaken claims before upload—executing peer critiques against deposited code and data overturned 49.4% of major headline conclusions that text-only AI reviewers missed.

Across AI science and enterprise software, the bottleneck has flipped: generating plausible outputs is now cheap, while verifying accuracy and sustaining multi-agent coherence remain scarce. GGACRUX Labs bridges virtual simulation research and dependable production software through three principles:

01 · Grounding
Environment-grounded collaboration

Coordinating specialized agents through shared world state and explicit communication protocols to prevent reasoning-action mismatch.

02 · Audit
Executable verification

Replacing prose-only reviews with sandboxed checks and claim ledgers that detect claim drift before summaries outrun evidence.

03 · Oversight
Human epistemic stewardship

Pairing agents' strengths in systematic execution and replication with human judgment over rules, risk boundaries, and real-world meaning.

2 · Failure dynamics

Why multi-agent LLM systems fail (MAST taxonomy)

Audits of more than 150 multi-agent conversation traces (Cemri et al., NeurIPS 2025) demonstrate that organizational design flaws and inter-agent misalignment account for over three-quarters of system breakdowns. Upgrading the underlying model does not fix structural coordination errors.

44.2% of failures
System design issues

Flawed task decomposition, missing termination caps (12.4%), and infinite step repetition (15.7%) caused by unbounded orchestration loops.

32.35% of failures
Inter-agent misalignment

Sub-agents acting on conflicting assumptions or exhibiting reasoning-action mismatch (13.2%), where execution contradicts stated plans.

23.5% of failures
Verification failures

Pipelines accepting superficial syntactic checks (e.g., code compiles) without runtime semantic validation, letting target leaks pass downstream.

3 · Reference stack

Four-layer architecture bridging simulation and software

Synthesizing GGACRUX Labs' embodied simulation framework (world E, state s_t, and operator rules θ_t) with late-2026 verification architectures yields a four-layer engineering stack:

Layer Component Mechanism & Safeguard
Layer 04 · Governance Human epistemic stewardship Accountable human operators define world parameters θ_t, set risk and budget guardrails, measure causal attribution via the Maker Index M_A, and judge real-world significance.
Layer 03 · Audit Executable verification & claim ledgers Isolated verifier agents run sandboxed tests and data scripts against deposited artifacts, binding every summary claim to raw outputs to prevent claim drift.
Layer 02 · Protocol Institutional roles & diversity Enforces hard loop caps, masks intermediate peer scores (preventing 88% crowding collapse), mixes distinct model families, and separates planner, executor, and skeptic roles.
Layer 01 · Grounding Embodied & computational sandbox Shared state environments—from 3D block worlds with heterogeneous senses (vision, language, RL) to reproducible code repositories—anchoring actions in verifiable state transitions.
4 · Literature review

Prior work and why it matters here

Each entry summarizes what the study found and how it informs GGACRUX Labs' research program. Filter by research stream below:

A Society of AI Scientists (Ahmad et al., alphaXiv, Nov 2026) Verification & societies

936 autonomous agents across 35 labs produced 936 studies over 12 weeks via an MCP server with claim ledgers and peer review. Numeric fidelity reached 93.8%, yet code execution overturned 49.4% of major headline conclusions that text-only reviewers missed.

Why it matters here: proves that prose-only peer review creates false confidence and establishes executable verification as mandatory infrastructure.

A Society of Researchers (Asaria et al., Transformer Lab Canada, arXiv, Oct 2026) Verification & societies

10,000 persistent AI researchers governed by explicit institutions—a human Mayor allocating compute grants without assigning tasks, alongside Maverick, Follower, and Skeptic roles.

Why it matters here: demonstrates that structural institutional rules outlast fragile prompt instructions and directly parallels GGACRUX Labs' separation of human rules from agent policies.

Scientist World (Lu et al., Princeton, arXiv, Nov 2026) Verification & societies

Converts 1,000 arXiv papers into self-contained executable repositories managed by server-side compute caps (Nexus) and distributed workers (Hive), lifting small-model ideation by 158% through execution-grounded traces.

Why it matters here: provides a concrete blueprint for grounding agent iteration in runnable sandboxes rather than unverified text synthesis.

Xcientist: Making AI Scientists Auditable (arXiv, 2026) Verification & societies

Maintains a shared evidence graph linking every design revision back to source passages and completed runs, cutting claim drift from 36.8%–50.0% down to 3.6%–16.7%.

Why it matters here: supplies the provenance graph pattern needed to keep final reports tethered to raw experimental artifacts.

Project Sid / PIANO (Altera.AL et al., arXiv, Nov 2024) Embodied & social worlds

10 to 1,000+ LLM agents in Minecraft developed specialized roles (3.41 vs. 2.60 bits entropy), voted on collective taxes, and diffused cultural norms via parallel neural orchestration.

Why it matters here: the strongest existing evidence that embodied agents build social structure—yet descriptive rather than causal, motivating GGACRUX Labs' Maker Index.

Concordia (Vezhnevets et al., 2023) & AgentSociety (Piao et al., 2025) Embodied & social worlds

Concordia introduces a logged Game Master mediating physical state; AgentSociety scales to 10,000+ agents to test policy-shock interventions.

Why it matters here: validates logging operator interventions separately from agent actions to measure causal world authorship.

Why Do Multi-Agent LLM Systems Fail? / MAST (Cemri et al., NeurIPS 2025) Failure engineering

Audited 150+ multi-agent traces across 14 failure modes, finding 44.2% system design flaws, 32.35% inter-agent misalignment, and 23.5% verification failures, with 17.2× error amplification in unchecked topologies.

Why it matters here: proves that reliability comes from orchestration topology, termination bounds, and independent verification rather than model scale alone.

A Record for Agent Societies in Knowledge Time (Hall, alphaXiv, Nov 2026) Verification & societies

Analyzes pre-publication retractions (such as agent Cedar withdrawing a 13.2-point gain after a control experiment) and proposes immutable, time-stamped reliance records.

Why it matters here: highlights the need for auditable revision histories when downstream agents build upon upstream claims.

5 · Key terms

Terminology calibration

  • Multi-agent orchestration. Coordinating heterogeneous specialized agents through defined interaction topologies, task decomposition, and communication protocols—so specialist minds collaborate, critique, and test ideas rather than overloading one model.
  • Environment grounding. Coupling agent perception, memory, and action loops to verifiable physical, 3D virtual, or executable computational state instead of text-only chat.
  • Maker index (M_A). GGACRUX Labs' Shapley-value attribution metric running from 0 to 1 across four seeded counterfactual replays, measuring how much of a world outcome stems from agent policies versus human rules.
  • Institutional governance. Enforcing structural rules, resource budgets, persistent reputations, and role diversity at the system layer rather than relying on fragile prompt instructions.
  • Reasoning-action mismatch. A MAST failure mode (13.2% of observed breakdowns) where an agent's executed action contradicts its own stated reasoning plan.
  • Executable verification. Validating agent claims by programmatically running critiques, unit tests, or data scripts against deposited code and raw artifacts rather than trusting prose summaries.
  • Claim drift. An unresolved divergence between a published conclusion and its underlying experimental evidence or component revisions.
  • Human-in-the-loop epistemic stewardship. Retaining accountable human authority over problem selection, world parameters θ_t, risk boundaries, and evaluation of real-world significance.
